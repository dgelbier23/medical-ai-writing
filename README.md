# Medical AI Writing

**A model can score well on every benchmark and still not be safe to use on a patient.**

These articles are about the gap between how medical AI is *measured* and whether it can be
*trusted*: what current evaluation practice captures, what it misses, and what more careful
evaluation would look like. Each one starts from the published literature and asks the same
question: does the evidence actually support the claim?

## Articles

### [Synthetic Data in Medical Image Analysis: A Critical Evaluation](synthetic-data-medical-imaging/)

*Medical imaging research is short of data: patient scans are scarce, sensitive and tightly
governed. Synthetic data, meaning images that are augmented, simulated or generated by models such
as GANs and diffusion models, promises a way around all three. But how do we know it is good enough
to train a clinical model on?*

This article maps the field, then tests two influential studies against it.

- **A map of the field:** how synthetic data is produced (augmentation, simulation, generation,
  hybrids), where it enters the training pipeline (augmentation, rebalancing rare classes,
  pre-training, domain adaptation), and how it is evaluated.
- **A critique of two contrasting studies:** [Gao et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-023-00629-1),
  which uses large-scale simulation mainly to *replace* scarce X-ray data, and
  [Al Khalil et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2022.102688), which uses GANs mainly
  to *complement* real cardiac MRI data.
- **The central argument:** most studies judge synthetic data by the performance gains it brings
  (accuracy, DICE), yet better scores do not show that the data is clinically realistic, that the
  model will generalise to new hospitals, or that patient privacy is protected. Without
  standardised evaluation, external validation and clinicians in the loop, reported gains cannot
  be taken as clinical progress.

## The thread through this work

The same principle runs through my technical projects: **a single performance number is not
evidence of reliability.** In [dental-3d-vae](https://github.com/dgelbier23/dental-3d-vae), my MSc
dissertation, a generative model that completes incomplete 3D tooth scans is evaluated not only on
how accurate its completions are but on how *consistent* they are, and the two turn out to be only
weakly related. This writing is the literature-facing side of that idea.
